### GST 2.0 — Key Impact Areas and Way Forward for Business

**Executive Summary**
The 56th GST Council Meeting held on 3 September 2025 approved a fundamental restructuring of India's GST rate architecture — collapsing the existing four-tier structure into a simplified framework of three rates: 5% (merit), 18% (standard), and 40% (de-merit). Rate changes on goods (except tobacco products) and all services take effect from 22 September 2025, giving businesses under three weeks to reconfigure pricing, systems, and contracts.

## Current Legal Position

India's GST framework currently operates on a four-slab rate structure: 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%. A Compensation Cess applies over and above the 28% slab on specified de-merit and luxury goods — automobiles, tobacco products, aerated beverages, and coal — to fund the states' revenue shortfall guarantee under Section 18 of the Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016. The cess was originally time-limited to five years (July 2017 to June 2022) but was extended to March 2026 to service back-to-back loans raised during the pandemic.

Input Tax Credit (ITC) under Sections 16–21 of the CGST Act, 2017 permits set-off of tax paid on inputs and input services against output tax liability. Where the rate on inputs exceeds the rate on outputs (inverted duty structure), Section 54(3)(ii) provides for refund of accumulated ITC, subject to Formula A in Rule 89(5). Exempt supplies attract proportional ITC reversal under Section 17(2) read with Rule 42/43. This ITC architecture creates direct financial consequences whenever rates shift — every rate reduction alters the ITC equilibrium across the supply chain.

## Proposed Changes

- **Three-Slab Consolidation:** The 12% slab is eliminated entirely. All goods and services move to 5% (merit), 18% (standard), or 40% (de-merit). Previously, four active rate slabs existed at 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%.

- **FMCG Mass Reductions:** Hair oil, toilet soap bars, shampoos, toothbrushes, toothpaste, bicycles, tableware, kitchenware, and other household articles reduced to 5%. Previously 12% or 18%.

- **Food Items — Near-Universal 5%:** Packaged namkeens, bhujia, sauces, pasta, instant noodles, chocolates, coffee, preserved meat, cornflakes, butter, and ghee reduced to 5%. Previously 12% or 18%.

- **Food Items — NIL Rate:** UHT milk, prepackaged chena/paneer, and all Indian breads (chapati, roti, paratha, parotta) reduced to NIL. Previously 5%.

- **Automobiles — 28% to 18%:** Small cars, motorcycles up to 350cc, three-wheelers, buses, trucks, and ambulances reduced to 18%. Air-conditioning machines, TVs up to 32 inches, and dishwashing machines also move from 28% to 18%. Previously 28%.

- **Auto Parts Uniformity:** All auto parts attract a uniform 18% regardless of HS code. Previously rates varied between 18% and 28% depending on classification.

- **Cement:** Reduced from 28% to 18%. Previously 28%.

- **Pharmaceuticals — Lifesaving Drugs:** 33 lifesaving drugs reduced from 12% to NIL; 3 additional drugs for cancer and rare diseases reduced from 5% to NIL. All other drugs reduced from 12% to 5%. Previously 5% or 12%.

- **Medical Devices:** Apparatus for medical, surgical, dental, or veterinary use reduced from 18% to 5%. Diagnostic kits, reagents, glucometers, bandages, and gauze reduced from 12% to 5%. Previously 12% or 18%.

- **Insurance — Individual Policies Exempt:** All individual life insurance policies (term, ULIP, endowment) and all individual health insurance policies (including family floater and senior citizen) along with reinsurance thereof are now exempt from GST. Previously 18%.

- **Textiles — Inverted Duty Correction:** Manmade fibre reduced from 18% to 5%; manmade yarn reduced from 12% to 5%. Previously 18% (fibre) and 12% (yarn), creating a long-standing inverted structure against 5% fabric.

- **Fertilizers — Inverted Duty Correction:** Sulphuric acid, nitric acid, and ammonia reduced from 18% to 5%. Previously 18%, against 5% on finished fertilizers.

- **Agriculture Machinery:** Tractors, soil preparation machinery, harvesting and threshing equipment, straw balers, and composting machines reduced from 12% to 5%. Previously 12%.

- **Renewable Energy:** Devices and parts for renewable energy reduced from 12% to 5%. Previously 12%.

- **Hotel Accommodation:** Rooms up to Rs. 7,500 per unit per day reduced from 12% to 5%. Previously 12%.

- **Beauty and Wellness Services:** Gyms, salons, barbers, yoga centres reduced from 18% to 5%. Previously 18%.

- **Tobacco Products — Status Quo:** Pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, chewing tobacco, zarda, unmanufactured tobacco, and bidi continue at existing GST and compensation cess rates until all loan and interest obligations under the compensation cess account are discharged. The Finance Minister will separately notify the transition date.

- **Tobacco Valuation — RSP Basis:** GST on pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, and chewing tobacco will be levied on Retail Sale Price instead of transaction value. Previously assessed on transaction value.

- **Phased Implementation:** Services and all goods (except tobacco products) effective 22 September 2025. Tobacco products transition date to be separately notified by the Finance Minister.

- **IDS Provisional Refunds:** 90% provisional refund for inverted duty structure claims, on risk-assessment basis, mirroring the existing zero-rated supply refund mechanism. Operationalized from 1 November 2025 via administrative instructions pending CGST Act amendment.

- **GSTAT Operationalization:** Accepting appeals before end of September 2025; hearings commence before end of December 2025. Limitation for backlog appeals set at 30 June 2026.

## Sector-Specific Impacts

**Automobile**
Rate on small cars and motorcycles (up to 350cc) drops from 28% to 18%, a 10-percentage-point reduction that directly lowers ex-showroom prices. Buses, trucks, ambulances, and three-wheelers see the same reduction. All auto parts move to a uniform 18%, eliminating classification disputes between HS chapters that previously attracted different rates. Compensation cess on automobiles is not mentioned as removed — manufacturers must verify whether cess continues to apply alongside the reduced base rate. OEMs and dealers must reprice entire model catalogues and update dealer management systems before 22 September 2025. Transitional stock in dealer yards at old rates will need credit note or price adjustment treatment under existing agreements.

**Insurance**
Individual life and health insurance policies become fully exempt, including family floater and senior citizen health policies. Reinsurance on individual policies is also exempt. Group insurance policies (employer-sponsored health, group term life) are not mentioned as exempt and remain taxable at the standard rate. Insurers must immediately segregate individual and group policy portfolios for differential GST treatment. Section 17(2) ITC reversal kicks in for the exempt individual policy business — insurers must compute proportional reversal under Rule 42/43 for shared inputs and input services. Premium collection systems need reconfiguration to stop charging GST on individual policies from 22 September 2025. Pending claims for ITC on individual policy costs in the supply chain (brokers, TPAs) must be evaluated for reversal.

**Pharmaceuticals**
36 lifesaving drugs move to NIL rate. All remaining drugs compress from 12% to 5%. Medical devices and diagnostic equipment drop to 5%. The rate reduction on finished drugs to 5% while many Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs) and chemical intermediates were already at 18% deepens the inverted duty structure for pharma manufacturers. ITC accumulation will accelerate. The new 90% provisional refund mechanism for IDS claims (from 1 November 2025) partially addresses the cash-flow lock-up, but manufacturers must file refund claims actively and maintain clean documentation for risk-based assessment. MRP on drug packaging requires revision under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order and DPCO ceiling price recalculations by NPPA.

**Construction**
Cement drops from 28% to 18% — a direct cost reduction for real estate developers and infrastructure projects. Works contract services (currently 18% on immovable property, 12% on government works) will need reassessment under the new slab structure since the 12% slab ceases to exist. Ongoing contracts with price escalation clauses tied to GST rates require immediate review. Developers operating under the 1%/5% concessional scheme (without ITC) see no direct benefit from the cement reduction, but developers outside the concessional scheme gain significant ITC headroom. The reduction improves project economics for infrastructure and commercial real estate where full ITC is available.

**FMCG**
The most sweeping rate reductions by volume. Everyday items — soaps, toothpaste, shampoos, cooking oils — compress to 5%. Food products including butter, ghee, chocolates, noodles, sauces, and namkeens move to 5%. UHT milk, paneer, and Indian breads become NIL-rated. MRP relabeling is mandatory under the Legal Metrology Act where the effective price reduces — companies must plan label reprints for lakhs of SKUs before 22 September 2025. Anti-profiteering provisions under Section 171 (now administered by CCI) require commensurate price reductions to be passed to consumers. FMCG companies operating on thin margins at 5% output with 18% input rates on packaging, advertising, and logistics will face ITC accumulation. Distributors holding pre-22-September stock at old rates face margin compression unless contractual pass-through mechanisms exist.

**Logistics**
Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services currently operate under two options: 5% without ITC or 12% with ITC. With the 12% slab eliminated, GTAs must transition to either 5% (merit) or 18% (standard). GTAs that opted for 12% with ITC face a binary choice — absorb the ITC loss at 5% or pass through the rate increase at 18%. The option exercise mechanism under Notification 11/2017-CT(R) needs fresh notification for the revised slab structure. Fleet operators must recalculate freight economics. E-way bill and transporter ID systems require no structural change, but invoicing values shift.

## Action Items

1. Map every active HSN and SAC code in your ERP to the revised rate — flag items moving between slabs and items becoming exempt. Complete by 15 September 2025.
2. Reconfigure GST tax engines, billing software, and e-invoicing templates to reflect three-slab rates (5%, 18%, 40%) before 22 September 2025.
3. Run ITC impact simulations for each business vertical — identify where output rate reductions create new inverted duty positions or convert taxable supplies to exempt.
4. Compute Section 17(2) proportional ITC reversal for any supply category moving to exempt status (particularly individual insurance policies, NIL-rated food items, and NIL-rated drugs).
5. Review all long-term supply contracts, works contracts, and rate agreements with customers and vendors for GST rate change clauses — issue renegotiation notices where contracts lack automatic pass-through.
6. Relabel MRP on all affected packaged goods under Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules — coordinate print timelines with packaging vendors immediately.
7. Prepare anti-profiteering documentation showing commensurate price reduction calculations for every SKU where GST rates drop. Retain working papers for CCI scrutiny.
8. File accumulated ITC refund claims under Rule 89(5) for existing inverted duty positions before the rate change date — post-change refund eligibility may alter.
9. Reassess GTA service option (5% without ITC vs. 18% with ITC) based on actual input cost structure and file fresh option declaration within the notification timeline.
10. Verify compensation cess applicability on automobiles post-rate reduction — the press release does not confirm cess removal alongside the base rate cut to 18%.
11. Prepare GSTAT appeal filings for pending disputed demands — appeals will be accepted from September 2025 with backlog limitation of 30 June 2026.
12. Reconcile transitional stock across the supply chain — goods purchased at old rates but sold post-22 September at new rates require credit note or differential pricing treatment.

## Practitioner Insights

**ITC Accumulation Risk.** The most consequential systemic risk from this reform is ITC pile-up. Where output rates drop to 5% but key input categories (packaging at 18%, advertising at 18%, professional services at 18%, capital goods at 18%) remain unchanged, the credit-to-liability ratio inverts sharply. Pharma, FMCG, and textiles are the primary affected sectors. The IDS refund mechanism helps, but Rule 89(5) formula caps refunds at the ratio of inverted inputs to net ITC — businesses with mixed supplies or exempt outputs get a reduced refund quantum. Cash-flow modelling must account for refund processing delays even with the 90% provisional mechanism.

**Section 18(4) Reversal on Newly Exempt Supplies.** Where goods or services transition from taxable to exempt (individual insurance, UHT milk, paneer, Indian breads, 36 lifesaving drugs), Section 18(4) read with Rule 44 requires reversal of ITC on inputs held in stock, inputs in semi-finished goods, inputs in finished goods, and capital goods, as on the date of exemption. Insurers, dairy processors, and pharma companies must conduct physical stock counts on 21 September 2025 and compute reversal amounts. The reversal is via ITC-03 filing. Missing this reversal triggers interest under Section 50 and potential demand under Section 73/74.

**Working Capital Disruption.** Businesses that were net ITC consumers (output rate > input rate) now become net ITC accumulators (output rate < input rate). The cash previously available from net tax collection now becomes locked-up credit. Treasury teams must reforecast monthly GST cash flows. For capital-intensive sectors transitioning from 28% to 18% output (automobiles, cement), the reduction in output liability shrinks the ITC absorption capacity — accumulated credit balances from capital expenditure will take longer to utilize.

**ERP and Billing System Overhaul.** The 22 September 2025 deadline leaves approximately 19 days from announcement. Tax determination logic in SAP, Oracle, Tally, and custom ERP systems must be updated with new HSN-rate mappings. E-invoicing schemas (IRN generation) pull rates from master data — stale masters will generate incorrect IRNs and trigger validation failures on the GST portal. Test all invoice flows in sandbox before go-live. Coordinate with GSP/ASP providers for API updates.

**State Industrial Incentive Recalibration.** Several states offer investment incentives structured as GST reimbursements — typically a percentage of SGST paid. Where output rates drop, the absolute incentive amount reduces proportionally even if the percentage stays constant. Businesses relying on state incentive schemes (particularly in manufacturing SEZs and industrial parks) must recalculate the net present value of incentive packages and engage with state industrial development authorities for revised terms.

**GSTAT Preparation.** With GSTAT accepting appeals from September 2025 and hearings from December 2025, the backlog limitation date of 30 June 2026 creates a hard filing window. Every demand order, refund rejection, and adverse ruling from 1 July 2017 to date that was previously unappealable (due to GSTAT non-existence) now has a defined appeal window. Audit teams must inventory all such orders, compute duty-interest-penalty exposure, quantify appeal economics (likelihood of success vs. cost), and prepare appeal memoranda. The Principal Bench will also serve as the National Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling, replacing the dormant AAAR in several states — conflicting advance rulings across states can now be resolved.

**Contract Review and Renegotiation.** Rate reductions do not automatically flow through supply chains. Contracts with fixed consideration (inclusive of GST) give the supplier a windfall. Contracts with GST charged separately (exclusive basis) pass the reduction to the buyer automatically. Review every material contract — supply agreements, lease deeds, service agreements, works contracts, franchise agreements — and classify by pricing mechanism. Issue contractual notices under the change-in-law or tax-variation clause where available. Where no such clause exists, negotiate supplementary agreements. Anti-profiteering complaints from customers are a real risk for suppliers who fail to pass through reductions — the CCI's National Anti-Profiteering Authority function has been active and penalty exposure is the entire profiteered amount plus interest.
