### GST 2.0 — Anti-Profiteering

**Executive Summary**

The 56th GST Council has approved the most sweeping rate rationalization since 2017 — collapsing four slabs into a 5%/18% two-rate structure effective 22 September 2025 — yet the press release is entirely silent on anti-profiteering enforcement. With the NAA having ceased accepting new complaints from 1 April 2025 under Notification 19/2024-CT, the onus of passing on rate-reduction benefits falls squarely on industry, with no dedicated adjudicating body standing behind it.

## Current Legal Position

Section 171 of the CGST Act remains on the statute book and continues to mandate that any reduction in rate of tax or benefit of ITC shall be passed on to the recipient by way of commensurate reduction in prices. The National Anti-Profiteering Authority, during its operational tenure, passed over 500 orders — but never settled on a uniform methodology, oscillating between SKU-level and company-wide or product-line approaches depending on the bench and the facts. Notification 19/2024-CT extinguished the NAA's mandate for new investigations from 1 April 2025 and transferred residual pending cases to the Competition Commission of India under Section 171(2). The CCI, however, has neither the institutional infrastructure nor published procedural rules for GST anti-profiteering adjudication. The result is an enforcement vacuum: Section 171 creates a statutory obligation, but there is currently no adjudicating authority empowered to entertain fresh complaints arising from the 22 September 2025 rate changes. This gap is not accidental — the government appears to be relying on market forces and reputational pressure rather than regulatory enforcement to ensure passthrough.

## Proposed Changes

- **Unprecedented scale of rate reductions.** The Council has reduced rates across hundreds of tariff lines — FMCG staples (soaps, toothpaste, hair oil) from 18%/12% to 5%; food items (ghee, butter, chocolates, noodles) from 12%/18% to 5%; cement from 28% to 18%; small cars, motorcycles up to 350cc, and commercial vehicles from 28% to 18%; 36 lifesaving drugs to NIL; and services such as hotel accommodation below Rs 7,500 from 12% to 5%. The breadth of reductions makes industry-wide price monitoring practically impossible without a dedicated authority.

- **Countervailing input cost increases.** Several inputs face effective rate increases that erode the net benefit of output rate reductions. GTA and logistics services previously at 12% will now attract the standard 18% rate. The abolition of compensation cess on coal is offset by coal itself moving to 18% GST — a net increase for power-intensive manufacturers. Manmade fibre reduction to 5% corrects inversion but reduces the ITC pool available to downstream fabric manufacturers already at 5%.

- **Government's stated expectation of passthrough.** The press release frames rate cuts as directly benefiting the "common man" and "aspirational middle class" — implicitly expecting businesses to pass on the full reduction. No quantitative passthrough obligation or monitoring mechanism has been specified. The unstated assumption is that competitive markets will do the enforcement that the NAA once did.

- **No new enforcement mechanism announced.** The 56th Council recommendations contain no reference to anti-profiteering, Section 171, or any successor body to the NAA. The GSTAT operationalization (appeals by September, hearings by December 2025) does not extend to anti-profiteering jurisdiction. This silence is the single most significant omission for compliance planning.

- **Alternative enforcement routes remain open.** Consumers and competitors may invoke the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (unfair trade practice / misleading pricing) or the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (MRP declarations) as proxy enforcement tools. State-level consumer commissions have historically been receptive to GST-related price gouging complaints, though these lack the sectoral expertise of the NAA.

## Action Items

1. Map every output HSN/SAC to old and new rates and compute the gross rate-reduction benefit at SKU level before 22 September 2025.
2. Model the net benefit after adjusting for input rate increases (GTA, coal, cess abolition) and ITC reversals under Section 17(2) on newly exempt outputs.
3. Document the pricing methodology adopted — SKU-level, weighted average, or product-line — and retain working papers defensible before CCI or consumer forums.
4. Issue revised price lists or MRP declarations before the effective date; align ERP tax masters, billing systems, and e-invoice schemas concurrently.
5. Review existing inventory purchased at old GST rates to quantify transitional ITC benefit and reflect it in post-22 September pricing.
6. Monitor CBIC circulars and Finance Ministry notifications for any reintroduction of an anti-profiteering enforcement framework through subordinate legislation or executive instruction.

## Practitioner Insights

- **Net benefit is not arithmetic.** A product moving from 18% to 5% output does not yield a 13-point consumer benefit. If key inputs (transport, fuel, packaging) simultaneously move from 12% to 18%, or from cess-bearing to higher GST rates, the actual passable benefit may be 6-8 points. Firms that mechanically reduce MRP by the full output differential will erode margins without legal obligation to do so.

- **Section 17(2) ITC blocking amplifies complexity.** Outputs newly exempted (UHT milk, certain breads, 36 drugs) trigger mandatory ITC reversal on attributable inputs. The "benefit" to the consumer is the removal of GST on the output, but the supplier absorbs stranded ITC — creating a cost increase, not a reduction. Practitioners must model this before advising clients to reduce prices.

- **Compensation cess credit stranding is a real risk.** Tobacco products remain at existing rates with cess until loan obligations are discharged; other goods lose cess entirely. Businesses holding compensation cess credit in their electronic credit ledger on transition date may face stranding if no transitional credit mechanism is notified. Watch for a Section 140-style transition provision.

- **Pre-September discounts and the Section 171 question.** Suppliers who proactively reduced prices before 22 September — whether through trade discounts, promotional pricing, or volume rebates — face the question of whether such reductions count toward their Section 171 obligation. NAA precedent (Hindustan Unilever, 2019) required post-notification reductions specifically attributable to rate changes; prior commercial discounts were disregarded. With no active authority to adjudicate, this remains an open risk.

- **The NAA sunset is statutory, not constitutional.** Section 171 and the enabling Rule 122-137 of the CGST Rules remain unamended. The government retains full power to reconstitute an anti-profiteering authority — or vest jurisdiction in GSTAT — through a simple notification. The current enforcement gap should not be treated as permanent. Firms that fail to document their pricing rationale now will be vulnerable if retrospective scrutiny is ever enabled.
