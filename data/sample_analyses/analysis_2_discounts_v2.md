### GST 2.0 --- Discounts under GST

**Executive Summary**

The 56th GST Council has recommended omitting Section 15(3)(b)(i) of the CGST Act, eliminating the twin requirements of a pre-supply agreement and invoice-level linkage for post-sale discounts to qualify as deductions from transaction value. Correspondingly, Section 34 will be amended to anchor GST credit notes to Section 15(3)(b), and Circular 212/6/2024-GST --- the CA certificate mechanism --- will be rescinded. A separate clarificatory circular will address three contentious issues: non-reversal of ITC on financial credit notes, treatment of manufacturer-to-dealer discounts as additional consideration, and post-sale discounts disguised as consideration for promotional services.

## Current Legal Position

Section 15(1) of the CGST Act fixes transaction value as the taxable value, subject to adjustments under Section 15(3). Section 15(3)(a) permits deduction of trade discounts given at the time of supply, provided they are recorded on the invoice --- this provision is straightforward and rarely litigated. Section 15(3)(b), governing post-sale discounts, imposes two cumulative conditions: clause (i) requires that the discount be established per an agreement entered into before or at the time of supply and be specifically linked to relevant invoices; clause (ii) requires that the recipient reverse proportionate ITC attributable to the discount. Where either condition fails, the supplier cannot reduce taxable value via a GST credit note and must instead issue a financial or commercial credit note --- an accounting adjustment that carries no GST consequence and leaves the recipient's ITC undisturbed. Recognising the practical impossibility of invoice-level linkage in multi-tier distribution chains, CBIC issued Circular 212/6/2024-GST (June 2024), permitting suppliers to demonstrate compliance with Section 15(3)(b)(ii) through a certificate from a practising CA or CMA confirming recipient-side ITC reversal. Despite this workaround, the clause (i) requirement continued to generate persistent litigation, particularly in FMCG, pharmaceutical, and automobile sectors where volume-based, year-end, and staggered incentive structures make it commercially impossible to pre-agree discount quantum at the time of each supply.

## Proposed Changes

- **Omission of Section 15(3)(b)(i):** The Council has recommended deleting the requirement that a post-sale discount be established under a pre-supply agreement and linked to specific invoices. This removes the structural impossibility that drove most discount-related disputes. Suppliers will no longer need to demonstrate contemporaneous documentation at the time of each supply.

- **Amendment to Section 34 for credit notes:** Section 15(3)(b) will be amended to require that qualifying post-sale discounts be routed through a credit note issued under Section 34. Section 34 will be correspondingly amended to include a cross-reference to Section 15(3)(b), creating a single statutory pathway: GST credit note with mandatory ITC reversal by the recipient. The surviving condition under Section 15(3)(b)(ii) --- recipient ITC reversal --- remains the sole gatekeeper.

- **Rescission of Circular 212/6/2024-GST:** The CA/CMA certificate mechanism becomes redundant once clause (i) is omitted and credit notes are statutorily linked to Section 15(3)(b). The Council has recommended rescinding this circular in its entirety.

- **Clarificatory circular on three contentious issues:** The Council has recommended a separate circular addressing: (i) confirmation that ITC reversal is not required where a post-sale discount is given through a financial/commercial credit note (i.e., without reducing taxable value); (ii) whether a manufacturer's post-sale discount to a dealer constitutes "additional consideration" in the dealer-to-end-customer transaction under Section 15(2)(a); and (iii) whether post-sale discounts are recharacterised as consideration for promotional or marketing services performed by the dealer, triggering a separate supply.

## Action Items

1. Map all existing discount and incentive arrangements to determine which qualify for GST credit notes under amended Section 15(3)(b) and which remain financial credit notes.
2. Review and update ERP credit note workflows to ensure GST credit notes carry the Section 34 cross-reference and trigger automated ITC reversal requests via IMS.
3. Revisit pending assessments, SCNs, and appeals where Section 15(3)(b)(i) non-compliance is the sole ground --- prepare applications for withdrawal or favourable disposal once the amendment is notified.
4. Audit manufacturer-to-dealer incentive structures for "additional consideration" exposure ahead of the clarificatory circular; restructure agreements where the discount could be recharacterised as consideration for dealer services.
5. Monitor the notification date --- the press release does not specify an effective date for these amendments, and transitional credit notes issued between now and notification will still need to satisfy the existing conditions.

## Practitioner Insights

- **Resolution of the core litigation issue:** The deletion of Section 15(3)(b)(i) eliminates the factual impossibility at the heart of most discount disputes. Year-end volume incentives, turnover slabs, and staggered scheme payouts could never satisfy a "before or at the time of supply" agreement requirement tied to individual invoices. With clause (ii) as the sole surviving condition, the legal test shifts from documentation of intent to verification of ITC reversal --- a factual question resolvable through return data.

- **GST credit notes vs. commercial credit notes --- the distinction persists:** The amendment does not collapse the two categories. A financial/commercial credit note remains a pure accounting entry with no GST impact and no ITC reversal obligation. Only a GST credit note under Section 34 reduces taxable value and triggers mandatory recipient-side reversal. Suppliers must classify each discount instrument correctly; misclassification in either direction creates either revenue exposure or unnecessary ITC reversal.

- **IMS transition risk:** The amended framework assumes that recipient ITC reversal can be verified systemically through the Invoice Management System. Until IMS reconciliation is fully automated and the acceptance/rejection workflow is stable, there is an operational gap. Suppliers issuing GST credit notes should maintain parallel confirmation mechanisms --- particularly for high-value, low-frequency discounts where a missed IMS action by the recipient could trigger denial of the supplier's value reduction.

- **Manufacturer-to-dealer "additional consideration" risk:** The upcoming circular's treatment of manufacturer discounts to dealers as potential additional consideration under Section 15(2)(a) warrants close attention. If a manufacturer's discount to a dealer is treated as flowing to the end-customer transaction, the dealer's output tax liability on the dealer-to-customer supply could increase. Multi-tier distribution models in FMCG and auto sectors should stress-test their discount flow structures before the circular is issued.
