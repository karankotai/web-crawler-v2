### GST 2.0 — Key Impact Areas and Way Forward for Business

**Executive Summary**

The 56th GST Council, chaired by FM Nirmala Sitharaman on 3 September 2025, approved the most sweeping rate rationalisation since GST's inception — collapsing the four-slab structure into a two-rate framework (5% merit, 18% standard) with a 40% demerit rate. Changes take effect 22 September 2025 for all goods and services except tobacco products, which remain at existing rates (including compensation cess) until cess loan obligations are fully discharged.

## Current Legal Position

The GST framework operates on a 4-tier structure — 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28% — supplemented by compensation cess on demerit goods under the GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017. The 12% and 28% slabs have been persistent sources of classification disputes and inverted duty accumulation, particularly in textiles, fertilisers, and FMCG. ITC refunds under Section 54(3)(ii) for inverted duty structures have been slow, with provisional refunds limited to zero-rated supplies. The compensation cess, originally set to sunset in June 2022, was extended to March 2026 to service back-to-back borrowings undertaken during COVID.

## Proposed Changes

- **Rate Structure Consolidation:** The 4-slab architecture collapses to a 2-rate model — 5% (merit) and 18% (standard) — with a 40% demerit rate replacing the current 28% slab. The 12% slab is eliminated entirely; the 28% slab survives only for tobacco products pending cess loan discharge.

- **FMCG & Food Items:** Common-use goods (hair oil, toilet soap, toothpaste, shampoos, bicycles, tableware, kitchenware) move from 12%/18% to 5%. Packaged foods — namkeens, bhujia, sauces, pasta, instant noodles, chocolates, coffee, cornflakes, butter, ghee, preserved meat — uniformly drop to 5% from 12%/18%. UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer/chena, and all Indian breads (roti, paratha, parotta) move to NIL.

- **Automobiles & Durables:** Small cars, motorcycles up to 350cc, buses, trucks, ambulances, three-wheelers, air-conditioners, dishwashers, and TVs up to 32" all move from 28% to 18%. Auto parts are rationalised to a uniform 18% irrespective of HSN classification, resolving long-standing disputes on OEM vs. aftermarket classification. Cement drops from 28% to 18%.

- **Pharma & Healthcare:** 36 lifesaving drugs (cancer, rare diseases, severe chronic conditions) become NIL-rated — 33 from 12% and 3 from 5%. All remaining drugs and medicines reduce from 12% to 5%. Medical apparatus, surgical devices, diagnostic kits, glucometers, and bandages move to 5% from 12%/18%.

- **Services — Insurance, Hospitality, Beauty:** All individual life insurance (term, ULIP, endowment) and individual health insurance (including family floater and senior citizen policies) — along with reinsurance — become fully exempt. Hotel accommodation up to Rs. 7,500/night drops from 12% to 5%. Beauty, gym, salon, barber, and yoga services drop from 18% to 5%. Stand-alone restaurants are barred from self-declaring as "specified premises" to avail 18%-with-ITC.

- **Inverted Duty Corrections:** Manmade fibre drops from 18% to 5% and manmade yarn from 12% to 5%, finally resolving the textile sector inversion. Fertiliser inputs (sulphuric acid, nitric acid, ammonia) move from 18% to 5%. Renewable energy devices and parts reduce from 12% to 5%. Provisional IDS refunds at 90% — currently available only for zero-rated exports — will extend to all inverted duty refund claims from 1 November 2025.

- **Timeline & Tobacco Carve-Out:** Effective date: 22 September 2025 for all goods and services. Tobacco products (pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, zarda, unmanufactured tobacco, bidi) are carved out indefinitely at current rates plus compensation cess. The FM retains discretion on transition date once cess obligations are cleared. RSP-based valuation replaces transaction value for these products.

## Sector-Specific Impacts

**Insurance:** Full exemption on individual life and health policies eliminates the 18% GST on premiums. Insurers must evaluate whether to pass through savings or absorb them against ITC reversals under Section 17(2) for newly exempt supplies. Existing long-term policies invoiced pre-22 September will need point-of-taxation clarity.

**Automobiles:** The 28%-to-18% reduction on small cars and two-wheelers compresses effective rates by ~10 percentage points. OEMs and dealers must renegotiate RSP-linked contracts and recalibrate MRP stickers before 22 September. Uniform 18% on auto parts eliminates classification litigation but requires HSN code remapping in ERP systems.

**FMCG:** Convergence at 5% removes inverted duty accumulation that plagued manufacturers buying inputs at 18%. Transitional ITC on stock held as of 22 September needs careful inventory cut-off documentation. Pre-revision contracts with modern trade and e-commerce platforms require anti-profiteering-compliant price reductions.

**Pharmaceuticals:** NIL rating on 36 drugs triggers mandatory ITC reversal under Section 17(2) read with Rule 42/43; companies currently claiming full ITC on shared inputs must immediately bifurcate taxable and exempt turnover. Hospitals and diagnostic centres benefit from 5% on devices but lose proportional ITC on common services.

**Textiles:** MMF sector inversion — where fibre at 18% exceeded fabric at 5% — is eliminated. Accumulated ITC balances from pre-reform periods remain refundable under Section 54(3)(ii), and the new 90% provisional refund mechanism (from 1 November 2025) will accelerate cash recovery.

**Hospitality & F&B:** Hotels under Rs. 7,500/night at 5% lose ITC eligibility (assuming no-ITC scheme applies by default as with restaurant services). Stand-alone restaurants confirmed at 5% without ITC; the "specified premises" clarification closes the loophole exploited by premium standalone restaurants opting into 18%-with-ITC.

## Action Items

1. Map every active HSN/SAC code against the revised rate schedule and update ERP tax masters by 15 September 2025.
2. Quantify ITC reversal exposure under Section 17(2)/Rule 42-43 for supplies newly exempt (insurance, NIL-rated drugs) and submit reversal in September GSTR-3B.
3. Review all customer and vendor contracts containing GST-inclusive pricing to renegotiate terms before 22 September; document anti-profiteering compliance.
4. Perform physical inventory cut-off on 21 September for transitional credit documentation on rate-differential stock.
5. File pending IDS refund claims now — the 90% provisional refund mechanism operationalises 1 November 2025 and will apply to claims in pipeline.
6. Update e-invoicing and e-way bill configurations for revised rates; test end-to-end before go-live on 22 September.

## Practitioner Insights

- **ITC Accumulation & Inverted Duty:** The 2-rate structure eliminates future inversion for most sectors, but legacy ITC balances remain locked in the refund pipeline. The extension of 90% provisional refunds to IDS claims (Section 54(6) amendment, effective 1 November 2025) is significant — file pending claims before that date to enter the risk-based fast-track queue. Monitor CBIC's administrative instructions; the legal amendment trails the operational directive.

- **Section 17(2)/18(4) Reversal on Exempt Supplies:** Insurance companies and pharma manufacturers face immediate ITC reversal obligations on inputs and capital goods attributable to newly exempt outputs. For capital goods acquired within five years, Section 18(4) read with Rule 44 requires reversal on a reducing balance basis — identify affected assets and compute reversal amounts before September return filing.

- **ERP & System Readiness:** The 19-day window (3 September to 22 September) is the tightest in GST history. Tax masters, invoice templates, POS systems, e-commerce integrations, and e-invoicing schemas all need simultaneous updates. Prioritise SAP/Oracle condition record changes and run parallel billing cycles from 15 September to catch mismatches.

- **Contract Review Urgency:** GST-inclusive contracts without change-in-law clauses create immediate margin exposure — particularly in long-term supply agreements, government tenders, and insurance distribution arrangements. Issue change-in-law notices immediately where rate reductions must flow through; verify that newly exempt supplies do not trigger contract termination clauses tied to tax status.
