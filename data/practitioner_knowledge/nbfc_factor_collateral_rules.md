# NBFC-Factor Collateral Rules and Gold Inventory Pledge

## Overview

An NBFC-Factor is a Non-Banking Financial Company registered with RBI and also registered under the Factoring Regulation Act, 2011. NBFC-Factors are primarily engaged in the business of factoring — purchasing receivables (invoices) from sellers and providing immediate finance. This document covers the specific RBI directions applicable to NBFC-Factors regarding acceptable collateral, including whether gold inventory can be accepted as a pledge.

## NBFC-Factor — Regulatory Framework

### Primary Regulation
- **Factoring Regulation Act, 2011** — governs the registration and operations of factors
- **RBI Master Direction — Non-Banking Financial Company — Non-Systemically Important Non-Deposit taking Company (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016** — applies to NBFC-Factors as NBFCs

### Nature of Factoring Business

Factoring is the business of:
1. Purchasing receivables (trade invoices) from a seller at a discount
2. Providing immediate working capital to the seller
3. Collecting payment from the buyer on the due date
4. The receivable itself is the primary security — NOT any physical collateral

## Can an NBFC-Factor Accept Gold Inventory as Collateral?

### Short Answer: Not as Primary Business

An NBFC-Factor's core business is **factoring** (purchasing receivables). Accepting gold as a pledge from a borrower is a **lending activity**, not factoring. The permissibility depends on:

### 1. Factoring Regulation Act Restrictions

- Section 5 of the Factoring Regulation Act defines factoring as assignment of receivables
- The Act does not authorize factors to engage in general lending against physical collateral
- NBFC-Factors can provide **supplementary finance** against receivables but the primary asset must be a receivable

### 2. RBI Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) Framework

Under the SBR framework effective October 2022:
- NBFCs are classified into 4 layers: Base, Middle, Upper, Top
- NBFC-Factors typically fall in the **Base Layer** (unless they exceed Rs. 1,000 crore asset size)
- Base Layer NBFCs have fewer restrictions on activities but must comply with principal business criteria

### 3. Gold Lending Directions (RBI/2019-20/148)

The RBI Master Direction on Lending against Gold/Silver Collateral primarily applies to:
- Scheduled Commercial Banks
- Urban Co-operative Banks (Tier 3 and 4)
- NBFCs engaged in gold lending as a **significant activity**

For an NBFC-Factor:
- If gold lending is NOT its principal business, it can accept gold as **additional/secondary collateral** for a loan
- However, this would be a lending activity, not factoring
- The NBFC must maintain its **principal business criterion** — factoring must remain the dominant activity (>50% of financial assets must be factoring-related)

### 4. Specific Restrictions for NBFC-Factors

- **Cannot become a gold lending NBFC**: If gold-backed lending exceeds 50% of financial assets, the NBFC-Factor loses its factoring character
- **LTV if gold is accepted**: Must comply with RBI's 75% LTV ratio for gold jewellery collateral
- **Gold inventory (raw material) vs. gold jewellery**: RBI directions on gold lending specifically cover gold jewellery; gold as raw material/inventory may be treated differently — as general inventory financing with gold as collateral
- **Working capital against gold inventory**: Under the amended Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral Directions (September 2025, RBI/2025-26/84), need-based working capital finance CAN be extended where gold/silver is used as raw material in manufacturing — BUT this applies to banks and Tier 3/4 UCBs, NOT specifically to NBFC-Factors

### Summary Position

| Activity | Permitted for NBFC-Factor? | Conditions |
|----------|---------------------------|-----------|
| Factoring (purchasing receivables) | Yes — primary business | Must remain >50% of assets |
| Lending against gold jewellery | Technically possible as secondary activity | Must comply with gold lending directions, LTV 75% |
| Accepting gold inventory as additional collateral for a loan | Yes, as secondary collateral | Does not change the nature of the primary factoring business |
| Gold inventory as primary pledge for a standalone loan | NOT recommended | Transforms the NBFC into a gold lending entity, violating principal business criterion |
