# Job Worker ITC - 3-Year Limit and Deemed Supply Issues

## Overview

Under GST, goods sent to a job worker do not attract tax at the time of sending (Section 143). However, the ITC availed on inputs/capital goods sent for job work is subject to conditions, particularly the time limit for return of goods. If goods are not returned within the prescribed period, the transaction is treated as a deemed supply, creating significant tax and compliance implications.

## Legal Framework

### Section 19(5) — Inputs Sent for Job Work

- The principal manufacturer can avail ITC on inputs sent to a job worker
- **Condition**: Inputs must be received back (or supplied directly from job worker's premises) within **1 year** from the date of sending
- If not returned within 1 year, it is deemed to be a supply on the date the inputs were sent
- The principal must pay tax along with interest from the date of sending

### Section 19(6) — Capital Goods Sent for Job Work

- ITC on capital goods sent for job work can be availed
- **Condition**: Capital goods must be received back within **3 years** from the date of sending
- If not returned within 3 years, deemed supply provisions apply (same as inputs)

### Section 143 — Job Work Procedure

- Goods can be sent to a job worker without payment of tax (under a challan)
- The principal must maintain records of goods sent and received
- Goods can be supplied directly from the job worker's premises (subject to conditions)
- **E-way bill**: Required if goods value exceeds Rs. 50,000

## The 3-Year Capital Goods Issue

### Practical Problem

Many manufacturers send capital goods (moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures) to job workers that remain there for extended periods — often beyond 3 years. This triggers deemed supply provisions.

### Consequences of Breach

1. **Tax liability**: The principal must pay GST on the value of capital goods (as a supply)
2. **Interest**: Interest under Section 50 from the date the goods were sent
3. **ITC reversal**: The ITC claimed on those capital goods must be reversed
4. **Invoice requirement**: A deemed supply needs to be reported in GSTR-1

### Valuation

For deemed supply under Section 19(5)/(6), the value is determined under Section 15:
- Transaction value (if available)
- If no consideration, Rule 28 applies — open market value or cost + 10%
- For capital goods: Depreciated value based on straight-line method from the date of purchase

## Practical Remedies and Workarounds

### 1. Bring Back and Re-send

**Strategy**: Before the 3-year limit expires, physically bring back the capital goods to the principal's premises and re-send them on a fresh challan.

**Legal basis**: The time limit restarts from the date of fresh sending.

**Documentation needed**:
- Inward delivery challan showing receipt at principal's premises
- New outward delivery challan for re-sending
- E-way bill for both movements (if value > Rs. 50,000)
- Gate register entries at both premises
- Transporter receipt/lorry receipt

**Risk**: Tax authorities may treat this as a sham transaction if:
- No genuine physical movement occurred
- Back-to-back challans with the same transporter on the same day
- No evidence of inspection/maintenance at principal's premises

**Best practice**: Schedule the return during a maintenance/repair cycle so there is a genuine business reason for the return.

### 2. Pay Tax on Deemed Supply

If the capital goods cannot be practically returned, pay the tax:
- Calculate GST on the depreciated value of the capital goods
- Report as a supply in GSTR-1 (B2B if job worker is registered, B2C otherwise)
- Pay tax in GSTR-3B
- **Note**: The job worker does NOT get ITC on this deemed supply (no actual invoice)

### 3. Transfer of Title

If the capital goods are intended to permanently remain with the job worker, consider:
- Selling the capital goods to the job worker (actual supply with GST invoice)
- The job worker can claim ITC on the purchase
- This converts a deemed supply (adverse tax event) into a normal supply (routine)

### 4. Maintain Comprehensive Records

Even if no action is taken, maintain:
- Delivery challan register with dates of sending
- Acknowledgment from job worker
- Periodic reconciliation of goods at job worker's premises
- Physical verification reports (at least annually)

## Tracking System for CAs

Recommended tracker columns:
1. Capital goods description
2. Date sent to job worker
3. Job worker GSTIN and premises
4. Value at time of sending
5. Depreciated value (current)
6. 3-year deadline date
7. Status: With job worker / Returned / Re-sent / Deemed supply paid
8. Action needed before deadline

## Common Mistakes

1. **Not tracking the timeline**: The 3-year clock starts from the DATE OF SENDING, not the date of job work completion
2. **Sending without challan**: Goods sent without proper delivery challan cannot avail the Section 143 benefit
3. **Not reconciling**: Failing to periodically reconcile goods at the job worker's premises
4. **Confusing moulds with inputs**: Moulds and dies are capital goods (3-year limit), not inputs (1-year limit). Misclassification shortens the available time.
5. **Multiple job workers in chain**: If goods go from Job Worker A to Job Worker B, the timeline starts from when the PRINCIPAL sent the goods, not from when A sent to B
