# Health Insurance ITC and Blocked Credits under Section 17(5)

## Overview

Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 lists specific goods and services for which Input Tax Credit (ITC) is BLOCKED — meaning the recipient cannot claim ITC even if the supply is used in the course of business. Health insurance is one of the most commonly disputed items under this section.

## Section 17(5)(b) — The Blocked Credit Provision

**Section 17(5)(b)** blocks ITC on the following services when used for personal consumption of employees:

> "(b) the following supply of goods or services or both—
> (i) food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, leasing, renting or hiring of motor vehicles, vessels or aircraft...
> except where... (B) it is obligatory for an employer to provide the same to its employees under any law for the time being in force"

### Key Interpretation

The section blocks ITC on "health services" — but **health insurance** and **health services** are distinct. The GST Council and CBIC have provided the following clarifications:

## Scenario 1: Employer Procures Group Health Insurance Policy

### Is GST on the premium eligible for ITC?

**Answer: Generally NO** — blocked under Section 17(5)(b)

**Reasoning**:
1. Health insurance is treated as a "health service" under Section 17(5)(b)
2. The employer is procuring it for employees' personal benefit (health coverage)
3. ITC is blocked unless the employer is **obligated by law** to provide health insurance

**Exception — When ITC IS available**:
- If the employer is **legally obligated** to provide health insurance under any law (e.g., contract labour regulations, specific industry regulations)
- If the health insurance is provided as part of a **composite supply** where the principal supply is a taxable output supply (rare scenario)
- **Post-53rd GST Council meeting**: The Council recommended that ITC be available on health insurance taken for employees where it is a statutory obligation under the Employees' State Insurance Act (ESIC) or similar legislation

### Practical Position

- For most private sector employers: ITC on group health insurance premium is **blocked**
- For employers where ESIC does not apply (salary > Rs. 21,000/month): If they provide group mediclaim as a substitute welfare measure, ITC remains blocked as there is no statutory mandate
- The GST charged on the premium becomes a cost to the employer

## Scenario 2: Employer Reimburses Employees Who Buy Their Own Policy

### Is the reimbursement subject to GST? Is ITC available?

**Answer: No GST and no ITC question**

**Reasoning**:
1. The employee purchases the health insurance in their **individual capacity** — the insurer issues the policy and GST invoice to the employee, NOT the employer
2. The employer's reimbursement is a **salary/perquisite/benefit** — it is not a supply under GST
3. Since the employer does not receive a GST invoice for the insurance, there is **no ITC to claim**
4. The reimbursement is treated as employee compensation, taxable under Income Tax (as a perquisite) but outside the GST framework

### Key Difference from Scenario 1

| Factor | Employer Procures (Scenario 1) | Employee Buys + Reimbursement (Scenario 2) |
|--------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| GST invoice to | Employer | Employee |
| ITC available to employer | No (blocked under 17(5)) | No (no invoice to employer) |
| GST cost borne by | Employer (as non-creditable cost) | Employee (reimbursed, but no ITC) |
| Income tax treatment | Employer's business expense; taxable perquisite for employee | Reimbursement is taxable perquisite |

## Recent GST Council Developments on Insurance

The 53rd and 54th GST Council meetings discussed:
1. Reducing GST rate on health insurance premiums from 18% to a lower rate (under consideration)
2. Possible exemption for health insurance for senior citizens (recommended by Group of Ministers)
3. ITC availability for employer-provided health insurance remains blocked unless statutory obligation exists
4. Term life insurance bundled with health insurance — each component taxed at its applicable rate
