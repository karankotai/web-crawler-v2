# GSTAT (GST Appellate Tribunal) Filing Guide

## Overview

The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT) is the second appellate forum under GST (after the first appellate authority). GSTAT started becoming operational from 2024-25 onwards after a long delay since GST implementation. This document covers the filing procedure, bench structure, and practical considerations.

## GSTAT Structure

### Principal Bench (New Delhi)

- Hears appeals against orders of the Appellate Authority where the place of supply is in more than one state
- Also handles appeals related to classification and rate issues of national importance

### State/Area Benches

- Each state has one or more benches based on the caseload
- The bench jurisdiction depends on the location of the assessing authority

### State-Wise Bench Jurisdiction (as notified)

| State | Bench Location | Jurisdiction |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Rajasthan | Jaipur | All divisions of Rajasthan — Jaipur, Jodhpur, Ajmer, Bikaner, Kota, Udaipur |
| Maharashtra | Mumbai, Pune | Mumbai bench (Mumbai, Thane, Konkan); Pune bench (rest of Maharashtra) |
| Tamil Nadu | Chennai | All divisions of Tamil Nadu including Puducherry |
| Karnataka | Bengaluru | All divisions of Karnataka |
| Gujarat | Ahmedabad | All divisions of Gujarat including Daman/Diu/Dadra |
| Uttar Pradesh | Lucknow, Prayagraj | Lucknow bench (Western UP); Prayagraj bench (Eastern UP) |
| Delhi | New Delhi | NCT of Delhi (Principal Bench also sits in Delhi) |
| West Bengal | Kolkata | West Bengal, Sikkim, Andaman & Nicobar |
| Telangana | Hyderabad | Telangana |

**For a taxpayer in Rajasthan**: The Jaipur bench of GSTAT has jurisdiction over all appeals from Rajasthan, regardless of the division or zone of the assessing officer.

### Bench Composition

| Dispute Value | Bench Composition |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Up to Rs. 50 lakh (tax amount) | Single Member (Judicial or Technical) |
| Rs. 50 lakh to Rs. 1 crore | Division Bench (1 Judicial + 1 Technical) |
| Above Rs. 1 crore | Division Bench (1 Judicial + 1 Technical) |

**Note**: The value threshold refers to the disputed tax amount, not the total demand (which may include interest and penalty).

## Appeal Filing Procedure

### Step 1: Determine Eligibility

- Appeal lies against the order of the First Appellate Authority (Commissioner Appeals / Additional Commissioner)
- Time limit: **3 months** from the date of the appellate order
- **Condonation**: GSTAT can condone delay of up to 1 month beyond the 3-month period (total 4 months)

### Step 2: Pre-Deposit

Before filing the appeal, the appellant must deposit:
- **20%** of the disputed tax amount (in addition to the amount already paid under the first appeal pre-deposit of 10%)
- Total pre-deposit at GSTAT stage: **20% of disputed tax** (over and above what was paid at first appellate stage)
- Pre-deposit can be made through **electronic cash ledger** only (not ITC)

**Pre-deposit calculation example (Rs. 80 lakh demand)**:
- First appellate authority pre-deposit (already paid): 10% = Rs. 8 lakh
- GSTAT pre-deposit: 20% of disputed tax = Rs. 16 lakh
- Total pre-deposit for tribunal appeal: Rs. 16 lakh additional (Rs. 24 lakh cumulative)
- Remaining demand stayed pending appeal: Rs. 56 lakh (80 - 24)
- The 20% is calculated on the disputed tax amount only (excluding interest and penalty components)

### Step 3: File Form GST APL-05

- **Form**: GST APL-05 (Appeal to GSTAT)
- **Filing**: Online through GST portal (once the GSTAT module is enabled)
- **Interim arrangement**: Until online filing is available, physical filing with the GSTAT registry

### Step 4: Supporting Documents

1. Certified copy of the order being appealed
2. Proof of pre-deposit payment (challan/ledger extract)
3. Statement of facts
4. Grounds of appeal
5. Any additional evidence not submitted earlier (with application for admission)

### Step 5: Cross-Objection

The respondent (government) can file a cross-objection within **45 days** of receipt of the appeal.

## Practical Considerations

### 1. Filing Before GSTAT is Operational

**Problem**: In states where GSTAT benches are not yet fully operational, how to preserve appeal rights?

**Solution**:
- Section 112(3): Appeal can be filed within 3 months from the date the President/State President enters office
- This "saving clause" was inserted to protect taxpayers during the period GSTAT was not constituted
- **Action**: File a protective appeal (even if GSTAT is not hearing cases) to preserve limitation

### 2. High Court Alternative

Under Article 226, taxpayers can directly approach the High Court (writ jurisdiction) if:
- GSTAT is not functional in their state
- Constitutional/fundamental rights issues are involved
- Urgent interim relief is needed

**Note**: Once GSTAT is fully operational, HCs may decline to entertain writ petitions on factual matters.

### 3. Stay of Demand

- GSTAT can grant stay of the remaining demand (80% after pre-deposit) pending appeal
- Application for stay filed along with the appeal
- Stay is not automatic — must demonstrate prima facie case and balance of convenience
- Stay is typically granted for a period (6 months, renewable)

### 4. Monetary Threshold for Appeals

The government has prescribed monetary thresholds below which the department should NOT appeal:
- GSTAT: Disputed tax amount below Rs. 20 lakh (check latest instruction for current threshold)
- This applies only to the department's appeal — taxpayers can appeal for any amount

## Timeline

| Step | Timeline |
|------|----------|
| First Appellate Order received | Day 0 |
| Appeal filing deadline | Day 90 (3 months) |
| Condonation available | Up to Day 120 (4 months) |
| Cross-objection by respondent | 45 days from appeal receipt |
| First hearing | Typically 2-3 months after filing |
| Stay application disposal | Usually at first hearing |
| Final disposal | 6-18 months (estimate, varies by bench) |

## CA Action Items

1. **Track all first appellate orders** and their dates — the 3-month clock starts immediately
2. **Calculate pre-deposit**: 20% of disputed tax (verify what was already paid at first appeal stage)
3. **Assess GSTAT vs HC**: If GSTAT is not operational or the issue is purely legal, consider HC writ
4. **Prepare grounds of appeal carefully**: GSTAT is a fact-finding tribunal — raise all factual and legal points here
5. **Monitor GSTAT notifications**: Bench locations, operational dates, and procedural rules are being notified periodically
