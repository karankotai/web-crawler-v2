# Compensation Cess Stranded Credit - Legal Position and Practical Options

## Overview

GST Compensation Cess was levied on certain luxury and demerit goods under the GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017. Many taxpayers — especially manufacturers — have accumulated cess credit that cannot be utilized because their output cess liability is lower than the input cess credit. This "stranded credit" has no refund mechanism, creating a significant cash flow issue.

## What is Stranded Cess Credit?

Compensation Cess is levied on goods like:
- Pan masala, tobacco products
- Aerated beverages
- Motor vehicles (certain categories)
- Coal, lignite, peat

**The problem**: Input credit of cess paid on raw materials (e.g., coal used in manufacturing) can ONLY be set off against output cess liability. Cess credit CANNOT be used against CGST, SGST, or IGST liability.

When a manufacturer's output product does not attract compensation cess (or attracts cess at a lower rate), the input cess credit accumulates without any mechanism for refund.

## Legal Position

### Section 11 of GST Compensation Cess Act

The GST Compensation Cess Act does not provide any separate refund mechanism for accumulated cess credit. Section 11(2) states that provisions of CGST Act apply to compensation cess "mutatis mutandis" — but Section 54 of CGST Act (refund) explicitly excludes compensation cess from inverted duty refund.

### Relevant Amendments

- **Notification No. 19/2024-Compensation Cess** — extended the levy beyond the original 5-year period (July 2022) to March 2026 for repayment of state compensation loans
- Post-June 2022, no compensation cess is levied on supplies, but credit on inputs continues to accumulate for taxpayers who had stock

### Section 54(3) Exclusion

Section 54(3) of CGST Act allows refund of "any unutilised input tax credit" for inverted duty structure BUT excludes:
- Refund of compensation cess under inverted duty
- This was upheld by CBIC in Circular No. 135/05/2020-GST

## Pending Writ Petitions

Several High Courts have writ petitions pending on the issue of stranded cess credit:

1. **Various manufacturers in mining sector**: Argued that Section 18(4) read with Section 54 should allow refund of accumulated cess credit
2. **Core legal argument**: The cess is a tax, and denying refund of legitimately accumulated credit violates Article 14 (equality) and Article 265 (no tax without authority of law)
3. **Government's counter**: Cess is a special purpose levy, and the Compensation Cess Act consciously did not provide for refund

**Status**: As of 2026, no definitive Supreme Court ruling. Some High Courts have issued notices to the government.

## Practical Options for Taxpayers

### Option 1: Utilize Against Future Cess Liability

If the taxpayer expects future output supplies attracting compensation cess, the credit can be set off. However, with the cess expiry approaching, this window is closing.

### Option 2: Transfer via ISD

If the taxpayer has multiple registrations and one unit has output cess liability, consider Input Service Distributor (ISD) mechanism to distribute the cess credit. **Caveat**: ISD distribution of cess is contested — consult before implementing.

### Option 3: Legal Challenge (Writ Petition)

Filing a writ petition in the jurisdictional High Court is an option. Consider:
- Cost of litigation vs. amount of stranded credit
- Timeline: HC matters take 1-3 years typically
- Precedent: No clear favorable precedent yet
- Interim relief: Some HCs have directed the government to not reject refund applications pending final hearing

### Option 4: Write Off

If the credit amount is small and litigation is not economical, the credit may need to be written off as a business loss. Accounting treatment:
- Debit: P&L (Indirect tax expense)
- Credit: GST Compensation Cess Input Credit (Balance Sheet)

### Option 5: Representation to GST Council

Industry bodies have represented to the GST Council for a one-time refund mechanism. CAs can support their clients' industry associations in making these representations.

## CA Advisory Points

1. **Quantify the stranded credit**: Calculate the total accumulated cess credit across all periods
2. **Evaluate litigation economics**: Compare credit amount vs. legal costs and timeline
3. **Watch for notifications**: The GST Council may announce a one-time settlement scheme
4. **Accounting treatment**: Even if contesting, consider provision/disclosure in financial statements
5. **Statute of limitations**: Refund applications under Section 54 have a 2-year limitation — file protective applications even while pursuing writ
