### 56th GST Council Meeting: Rate Rationalization and Trade Facilitation
**Circular Reference:** 56th GST Council Meeting | 03 SEP 2025 | Effective 22 SEP 2025 (in phases)

**In Brief:** The GST Council approved a shift towards a simpler tax structure with rate rationalization, aiming to benefit the common man, labor-intensive industries, and key economic drivers. These changes include rate reductions on numerous goods and services, implementation of a revised refund system, and measures to facilitate trade. The changes will be implemented in a phased manner starting 22nd September 2025.

## Key Changes

*   **GST Rate Structure Simplification:** The current four-tiered tax rate structure will be rationalized into a two-rate structure: a Standard Rate of 18% and a Merit Rate of 5%, with a special de-merit rate of 40% for select goods and services.
*   **Rate Reductions for Common Man Items:** GST is reduced from 18% or 12% to 5% on items such as hair oil, toilet soap bars, shampoos, toothbrushes, toothpaste, bicycles, tableware, kitchenware, and other household articles.
*   **GST Exemptions for Insurance:** GST is exempted on all individual life insurance policies (term life, ULIP, or endowment policies) and health insurance policies (including family floater policies and policies for senior citizens), along with reinsurance thereof.
*   **Rate Reductions for Food Items:**
    *   GST is reduced from 5% to NIL on Ultra-High Temperature (UHT) milk and prepackaged and labelled chena or paneer. All Indian breads (chapati, roti, paratha, parotta, etc.) will also be taxed at NIL rates.
    *   GST is reduced from 12% or 18% to 5% on food items such as packaged namkeens, bhujia, sauces, pasta, instant noodles, chocolates, coffee, preserved meat, cornflakes, butter, and ghee.
*   **Rate Reductions for Automobiles and Related Items:** GST is reduced from 28% to 18% on air-conditioning machines, TVs up to 32 inches, dishwashing machines, small cars, and motorcycles equal to or less than 350 CC. Uniform rate of 18% applies to all auto parts irrespective of their HSN code, and three-wheelers are reduced from 28% to 18%.
*   **Inverted Duty Structure Correction:** The inverted duty structure is corrected for the manmade textile sector by reducing GST on manmade fiber from 18% to 5% and manmade yarn from 12% to 5%. A similar correction is made in the fertilizer sector by reducing GST from 18% to 5% on sulphuric acid, nitric acid, and ammonia.
*   **Revised Refund System:** 90% of refund claims for zero-rated supplies and inverted duty structure will be sanctioned provisionally based on system-identified risk, effective 1st November 2025. The threshold limit for refunds arising out of exports made with payment of tax is removed.
*   **Simplified GST Registration:** An optional simplified GST registration scheme will be introduced, granting automated registration within three working days for low-risk applicants with an output tax liability not exceeding Rs. 2.5 lakh per month, effective 1st November 2025.
*   **GST on Pan Masala and Tobacco Products:** GST will be levied on Retail Sale Price (RSP) instead of transaction value on pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, unmanufactured tobacco, and chewing tobacco like zarda. Existing rates for these goods will continue until loan and interest payment obligations under the compensation cess account are completely discharged.

## Action Items

1.  Update HSN-rate mappings in billing systems to reflect the new GST rates for goods and services, effective 22nd September 2025, except for pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, chewing tobacco products like zarda, unmanufactured tobacco and bidi.
2.  Identify and categorize supplies based on the revised GST rates to ensure accurate tax invoicing and reporting.
3.  For businesses involved in zero-rated supplies or facing inverted duty structures, prepare for the revised refund system operational from 1st November 2025.
4.  Evaluate eligibility and consider opting for the simplified GST registration scheme, if applicable, from 1st November 2025.
5.  Small suppliers making supplies through e-commerce operators should monitor updates regarding the simplified GST registration mechanism.
6.  Businesses providing intermediary services should note the omission of clause (b) of section 13(8) of the IGST Act 2017 and adjust place of supply determination accordingly.
7.  Review and amend agreements related to post-sale discounts to align with the amended section 15(3)(b) of the CGST Act, 2017.
8.  Monitor for the notification of the actual date of transition to the revised rates of GST for pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, chewing tobacco products like zarda, unmanufactured tobacco and bidi.

## Additional Notes

The press release does not explicitly address transitional provisions for the rate changes. Businesses should prepare for potential challenges related to:

*   **Stock Valuation:** Determine the GST implications on existing stock as of 22nd September 2025, especially for items with rate changes. Consider the need for stock counts and potential Section 18 reversals.
*   **Input Tax Credit (ITC) Adjustments:** Analyze the impact of rate changes on ITC eligibility, particularly for goods moving from taxable to exempt categories (insurance) or experiencing significant rate reductions.
*   **ERP System Updates:** Plan for comprehensive updates to ERP systems to accommodate the new GST rates, HSN code mappings, and invoicing requirements.
*   **Anti-Profiteering Measures:** Be aware of potential scrutiny under anti-profiteering provisions, ensuring that the benefits of rate reductions are passed on to consumers.
*   **Valuation Rule Alignment:** The circular mentions aligning valuation rules with the change in the tax rate applicable to lottery tickets. Taxpayers dealing in lotteries should closely monitor these amendments to the GST Valuation Rules to ensure accurate valuation and compliance.
